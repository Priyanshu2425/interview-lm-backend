One Credit is one US cent of provider cost.
Not a house currency and not an average, but the exact figure the provider billed for your call. A call that cost us $0.21 spends 21 Credits. This is why the same Topic costs different amounts on DeepSeek, Gemini and Claude, and why choosing a cheaper provider visibly stretches a balance.

When something goes wrong

Two failures that look alike and are not. Which one you can see depends on which ledger you are on, and you will never be shown the other.

Your Credits ran out, and the Topic finished anyway
You're at −12 Credits. We let the Topic you were on run to the end rather than cutting it off: a half-examined answer would have left a worse record than none, and twelve Credits is the cheaper thing to lose. Top up and the Session picks up exactly where it stopped.
DeepSeek refused your key: it has no credit left
This is at your provider, not with us. Your OpenRouter key is valid but its balance is empty, so DeepSeek declined the call. Add funds at OpenRouter, or switch this Session to a provider your key can still reach. The Session is parked and nothing was lost.

Note what the second message does not say. You are on your own key, so you are spending no Credits, and telling you your Credits ran out would send you to fix something that isn't broken. The system has no path that produces that message for a key-holder.

Why only OpenRouter keys
An OpenRouter key carries its own spend cap and can be revoked on its own. A raw Anthropic, Google or DeepSeek credential can't, so we don't accept one, and there is no field here that would take it. Grading still runs on our side: if your machine produced the score, every record in the system would be forgeable.
