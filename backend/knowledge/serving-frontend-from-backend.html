The One-Door House 🏠

Why the same little server that answers your questions also hands you the picture book.

1 Imagine a lemonade stand

Your friend has a lemonade stand. It's just one little stand, with one window. You walk up to that same window whether you want to buy lemonade or just read the menu taped to the front.

One stand. One window. Everything happens right there.

2 Two ways to build a house

Now think about apps the same way. An app has two parts: the frontend (the pretty screen you tap and see, like the menu) and the backend (the brain that knows the answers, like whoever mixes the lemonade).

Left: one house, one door. Right: two houses that need a little bridge (a network call) between them.

3 "Serving the frontend from the backend"

That's the fancy grown-up phrase for the one-door house.

Instead of building the picture-book part (frontend) in its own separate house, the backend just keeps a copy of the finished picture-book pages in its closet. When someone knocks on the door and asks for a page, the backend hands over a page. When someone knocks and asks a real question ("how many candies do I have left?"), the backend answers that too — from the very same door.

Two houses The picture book lives in its own house. Every time it needs an answer, it has to run all the way to the other house and knock.
One house The picture book AND the answers live behind the same door. No running back and forth needed.

4 Why grown-ups sometimes like the one-door house

It's simpler to remember — there's only one address to visit. And since it's the same house, nobody has to explain to the guard at the door "hey, I promise I'm allowed to come in from that other house" — a rule called CORS that two-house apps have to deal with.

But sometimes grown-ups do want two separate houses — maybe the picture-book house needs to grow much bigger someday, on its own, without waiting for the answer-house to grow too. Both ways are fine! It's a choice, like deciding whether your lemonade stand and your cookie stand should be the same table or two tables.

5 The tiny trick behind the door

There's one more clever bit. Say someone knocks and asks for a page that doesn't exist yet in the closet — like page 47 of a book that only has 10 pages. A grumpy house would just say "nope!" and slam the door (that's a 404).

But the picture book has its own little map inside it that knows how to find any page once you're holding the book. So instead of slamming the door, the one-door house just hands over the cover of the book again, and lets the book's own map figure out page 47 by itself. That's the trick that makes deep links (like a bookmark to page 47) still work.

That's it — same door, different questions. 🏠
